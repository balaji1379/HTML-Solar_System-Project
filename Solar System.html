The solar system is a gravitationally bound system consisting of the Sun and all the celestial objects that orbit it.
  • The Sun is a massive star at the center that holds 99.8% of the system's mass .
  • Uses gravity to keep everything in orbit.
  • Eight Planets: Planets travel around the Sun in paths called orbits. They split into inner rocky planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) and outer gas/ice giants (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune).Dwarf Planets: Smaller planetary bodies like Pluto exist in the system, which do not clear their neighboring regions..
  • Named after the fleet-footed Roman messenger god, it is a world of extreme contrasts, featuring a heavily cratered surface resembling Earth's Moon and an exceptionally massive metallic interior.
  • Size and Mass: With a radius of 2,440 kilometres, Mercury is only slightly larger than Earth's Moon
  • It is the smallest major planet, being even smaller than massive moons like Jupiter's Ganymede and Saturn's Titan

  2. VENUS

    • Venus is the second planet from the Sun and the hottest planet in our solar system.
    • It is the second planet from the Sun, sitting at an average distance of about 67 million miles (108 million kilometers).
    • Runaway Greenhouse Effect: Venus's atmosphere consists of 96.5% carbon dioxide and 3.5% nitrogen, which traps solar heat with extreme efficiency.
    • Corrosive Clouds: The planet is permanently shrouded in thick, reflective clouds made of sulfuric acid droplets, hiding its surface from view.
    • Crushing Pressure: At the surface, the atmospheric pressure is a staggering 92 bars—nearly 90 times that of Earth, which is equivalent to the crushing pressure found 900 metres deep in Earth's oceans.

  3. EARTH

    • Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known celestial object in the universe to support life.
    • It is the third planet from the Sun (at an average distance of about 93 million miles or 149.6 million km).
    • An Ocean World: Liquid water covers 70.8% of Earth's surface. The remaining 29.2% consists of continental landmasses and islands. This makes Earth the only known planet capable of sustaining liquid surface water
    • Habitable Atmosphere: Unlike the crushing greenhouse atmosphere of Venus or the trace exosphere of Mercury, Earth's atmosphere features a balanced blend of 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, and 1% trace gases like argon and carbon dioxide.
    • Layered Structure: Earth's core is divided into a solid iron-nickel inner core reaching temperatures of 5,400°C, a liquid outer core that drives the planet's magnetic shield, a viscous rock mantle.

  4. MARS

    • Mars is the fourth planet from the Sun and a cold, rocky desert world known as the Red Planet.
    • It is the fourth planet from the Sun, sitting right after Earth.
    • Vast Volcanic Peaks: Mars is home to Olympus Mons, the largest volcano in the solar system. Standing over 25 kilometres tall, it is roughly three times the height of Mount Everest and spreads across an area nearly as large as the state of Arizona.
    • The Grandest Canyon: The Martian equator features Valles Marineris, an immense canyon system stretching more than 4,000 kilometres long. It cuts up to 7 kilometres deep into the crust, dwarfing Earth's Grand Canyon by comparison.
    • Size and Gravity: Mars is roughly half the diameter of Earth, with a radius of 3,390 kilometres. Because it lacks massive oceans, its dry surface area is almost equal to all of Earth's continents combined.

  5. JUPITER

    • Jupiter is the fifth planet from the Sun and the largest, most massive planet in our solar system.
    • It is the fifth planet from the Sun, located between Mars and Saturn.
    • Colossal Dimensions: Jupiter has an equatorial radius of 69,911 kilometres, making it roughly 11 times wider than Earth.
    • f Jupiter were a hollow shell, 1,000 Earths could easily fit inside. (Note: Precise radio measurements gathered by NASA's Juno mission redefined its exact parameters, revealing it is slightly more oblate—squashed at the poles—than previously estimated)
    • A Twelve-Year Orbit: Located roughly 778 million kilometres (5.2 AU) away from the Sun, it takes sunlight about 43 minutes to travel to Jupiter. It takes the giant planet 11.86 Earth years to complete a single journey around the Sun.

  6. SATURN

    • Saturn is the sixth planet from the Sun and the second-largest planet in our solar system.
    • Sixth planet from the Sun, orbiting at an average distance of about 1.4 billion kilometers (886 million miles).
    • Saturn's rings are the most extensive and visible in the solar system, spanning up to 282,000 kilometres across, yet they are astonishingly paper-thin, averaging only about 10 metres in thickness.
    • Composition: The rings are not solid structures; they are made of billions of individual particles ranging in size from microscopic dust grains to house-sized boulders.
    • These particles are almost entirely 99% pure water ice, with trace amounts of rocky debris and carbonaceous dust.

  7. URANUS

    • Uranus is the seventh Uranus planet from the Sun and an ice giant characterized by its unique sideways spin and blue-green color.
    • Seventh planet from the Sun, located about 2.87 billion kilometers (1.8 billion miles) away.
    • The defining trait of Uranus is its extreme axial tilt of 97.8 degrees. While most planets spin like spinning tops relative to their orbits, Uranus rolls along its orbital path like a bowling ball.
    • The Cause: Scientists heavily suspect that early in the planet's history, a cataclysmic impact with an Earth-sized object violently knocked Uranus completely onto its side.
    • Uranus is an ice giant, which means it is fundamentally different from gas giants like Jupiter or Saturn

  8. NEPTUNE

    • Neptune is the eighth and farthest known major planet from the Sun in our Solar System.
    • It is the eighth planet from the Sun, orbiting at an average distance of about 30.1 astronomical units (4.5 billion kilometers or 2.8 billion miles).
    • A Vibrant Azure Shroud: Like its twin planet Uranus, Neptune’s atmosphere consists mostly of hydrogen and helium. However, Neptune features a slightly higher concentration of methane gas, which absorbs red light wavelengths and reflects a much deeper, more striking azure-blue color compared to the pale cyan of Uranus
    • Driven by internal thermal heat radiating from deep inside the planet, Neptune has the most violent weather in the solar system.
    • Supercritical Ocean Mantle: Deep beneath its outer atmosphere lies a hot, high-pressure fluid mantle of water, methane, and ammonia slush. Intense pressures in this layer are strong enough to split methane molecules apart, generating a continuous diamond hail rain that sinks down toward its solid rocky core.
